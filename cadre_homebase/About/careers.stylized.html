Career Paths

Our innovative approach to art and technology has lead to world-class academic and career opportunities for our students. Current academic research partners include Intel Corporation, SRI International, Microsoft, Phillips LumiLED, VentureWell, and the John S. and James L. Knight Foundation. Graduating students have been placed at Silicon Valley technology companies including Adobe, Autodesk, Cisco, Google, Intel, Microsoft, and Electronic Arts, while DMA graduate students have gone on to teach at universities including UC San Diego, UC Santa Barbara, University of Nevada, Reno, and the National University of Singapore.



Career paths for Digital Media Art students include:

What DMA prepares you for

DMA is not a single “job pipeline”—it’s a skill stack that maps to many creative + technical roles. Your portfolio (and the story you tell about it) matters as much as the tools.

Common role clusters

Design + UX

  • UX/UI designer
  • Interaction designer
  • Design systems / prototyping
  • Motion + product visuals

3D + Motion

  • 3D artist / generalist
  • Motion designer
  • Technical artist (real-time)
  • Visualization / rendering

Creative Tech

  • Creative technologist
  • Front-end developer
  • Generative / data viz artist
  • Interactive installation developer

Games + XR

  • Game designer
  • Level / narrative design
  • XR prototyping
  • Gameplay / tools support

How to aim your portfolio

  • Pick a “through-line”: interaction, motion, 3D, code, or systems—and show growth
  • Document process: sketches, tests, failures, and decisions (not just final renders)
  • Ship something: a playable build, a live site, an install video, a published tool
  • Explain your role: what you owned vs. collaborated on
A helpful mindset

Employers hire for clarity. Make it easy to understand what you do and why it’s good.
